How Janet responds to the learning rate

A small language model trained over and over with a different feed-forward block each time, at a range of peak learning rates, then pushed past each block's best observed rate to see how and where it degrades.

TinyStories · 3.34 M parameters · 3,000 steps · 42 training runs on an Intel Arc integrated GPU

This is a first, exploratory comparison. Janet received more runs than the other blocks, and the classic blocks were not taken to their breaking point. Further measurements will follow with the same runs for every block: the same grid of rates, the same two seeds at every rate, and every block taken to its breaking point. Until then the results below are trends to be confirmed, not settled findings.

What was compared

A transformer language model alternates two kinds of block: attention, which mixes information across positions, and a feed-forward block, which transforms each position on its own. Almost all of a model's weights, and most of the reading of weights at inference time, sit in the feed-forward blocks. This study keeps the whole model fixed and swaps only that block.

Janet is the feed-forward block of the Janas project. Each of its units looks at its input through a few separate branches, each branch seeing its own part of the input, and the unit speaks only when its branches agree. When they do not, the unit is silent: its output is exactly zero and, at inference time, its weights need not be read at all. The silence is designed in.

The other three are the blocks used by today's language models. SwiGLU (LLaMA, Qwen, Mistral and most others) and GeGLU (Gemma) multiply two projections of the input, one passed through a smooth gate. Their units are practically never exactly silent. ReLU² (the squared rectifier, used for instance in Nemotron-H) zeroes negative values and squares the rest, so some of its units are silent on any given input, but not by design.

All four were given the same number of parameters (3.34 million in the whole model, within 0.01%). Each classic block uses the standard recipe from the literature: SwiGLU and GeGLU with a hidden width of 8/3 the model width (512 units), ReLU² with four times it (768). Janet has 752 units. None of Janet's own block settings were given to the others.

How it was measured

The model is small: width 192, 4 layers, 3 attention heads, a context of 128 tokens and a vocabulary of 4,096 tokens. Each run trains for 3,000 steps of 32 sequences of 128 tokens, about 12 million tokens of TinyStories. Every block uses the same settings: AdamW with weight decay 0.1, beta2 0.99 and gradient clipping at norm 1, a 200-step warm-up to the peak learning rate then a cosine decay down to 1e-4, and the same data order. These are the training program's defaults, set during Janet's development, and were not tuned for any block. The score is the cross-entropy loss on held-out text (lower is better), in nats per token.

Every block was trained at six peak learning rates from 1e-3 to 8e-3 with one seed; the grid steps are 1.33 to 2 times apart. Each block then got a second seed at its two best rates according to the first seed. Because Janet was still flat at 8e-3, it alone was taken higher, with two seeds per rate, until it broke (at 4.8e-2). So Janet has 18 runs, against 8 for each classic block, and two seeds at every rate from 6e-3 up where the others have them only at their two best.

Activity is measured in two ways. During training, the program records the share of unit-positions where a unit speaks, layer by layer: the speaking share of figure 5 and of the tables. After training, each saved Janet and ReLU² model was probed on 8,192 held-out positions. A unit counts as active if it speaks on at least 0.5% of them (41 positions), and as inactive otherwise; figure 4 gives the median share of positions where an active unit speaks. "Dead" below is shorthand for inactive in this sense: an inactive unit may still speak on a few positions. SwiGLU and GeGLU have no comparable measure, since their units never fall silent.

The optimum

At each block's best observed rate, averaged over two seeds, Janet reaches 2.020 (6e-3), ReLU² 2.061 (3e-3), SwiGLU 2.070 (4e-3) and GeGLU 2.084 (3e-3).

How large is the noise? Over every rate where a block has two seeds, the two differ by 0.003 to 0.016 for Janet and by 0.003 to 0.039 for the classic blocks. Estimated from these pairs, the standard deviation of a single run is about 0.007 for Janet and 0.018 for the classic blocks, and the standard error of the difference between two two-seed means about 0.013. Janet's 0.041 lead over ReLU² is about three standard errors: a clear trend, but not established with two seeds. Janet's runs also look less scattered than the others', but six pairs per side are too few to be sure. The three classic blocks are within 0.023 of each other and their order is not resolved.

Below the optimum the four lose alike: 0.04 to 0.07 at a third or a quarter of their best rate. At 1e-3, a sixth of its best, Janet is the worst of the four (2.165, against 2.118-2.133), and it is the only run in which clipping still acts at the end of training (43% of the last thousand steps). Janet needs its higher rate.

Past the optimum: two ways of degrading

Above its best observed rate, Janet degrades in two distinct regimes. Between 1.2e-2 and 3.2e-2 the loss rises gradually, by 0.015 to 0.035 per step up the ladder, steepening towards the top (from about 0.05 to about 0.11 per e-fold of the rate). Training stays stable: the gradient stays small and clipping does not act after the first thousand steps. At 4.8e-2 training becomes unstable in the usual way: the gradient grows roughly twentyfold, clipping acts on half the steps or more, and the loss ends at 3.4.

The gradual regime is where Janet's units become inactive.

The two pictures go together. Janet's loss is lowest at 6e-3 and 8e-3, indistinguishable within the seed spread, where fewer than 1% of the units are inactive. It starts to rise at 1.2e-2, where layer 2 has lost 9%, and the rise steepens from 2.4e-2, where layer 2 has lost 45-75% of its units depending on the seed. The units that stay active also speak less: in layer 2 the median active unit speaks on 10-11% of positions at the optimum and 3.5-4.6% at 3.2e-2.

Inactive units are not peculiar to Janet. ReLU², the other block whose units can fall silent, shows the same thing, and sooner: measured against each block's own best rate, at twice the optimum Janet has lost 9% of layer 2 and ReLU² 33%. In both, units that can be switched off become inactive when the steps are too large; Janet's resist longer.

One reading, consistent with figures 1 and 3 but not tested directly, is that Janet degrades gradually because it sheds capacity one unit at a time while the rest of the network carries on with the units that remain. What the classic blocks do above 8e-3 was not measured. Whatever the mechanism, the flat region above Janet's optimum has a cost that the loss alone hides; on a larger model or a longer run that loss of units could accumulate, and it has to be measured there.

Why do Janet's units become inactive?

Four explanations were on the table. The data rule out one and support one.

  • Clipping holds back the updates. Ruled out: below the breaking point clipping acts only early on, and less at high rates (figure 7).
  • Units go silent at high rates and stop learning. Supported only as the accompaniment of the gradual regime: the loss rises as units fall below the threshold (figures 3 and 4). Whether silent units stop learning, and what silences them, was not measured.
  • Janet's weights grow until the effective step stays the same whatever the nominal rate. Not yet tested: it needs the size of each group of weights through training. The falling gradient norm in every block (figure 7) is consistent with growing weights, but does not prove it.
  • Janet steadies the rest of the network. Attention and embeddings train at the same rate as the feed-forward block; if Janet's block keeps the signal tame, they too tolerate higher rates. Not yet tested: it needs one rate for the feed-forward block and another for the rest.

Limits

  • One small model and a short run: 3.34 M parameters, 3,000 steps, about 12 M tokens, one dataset and one tokenizer. This is far from a fully trained model, and a preference for high learning rates in short runs may not hold in long ones. The differences may shrink or grow with size and length.
  • Unequal runs: Janet had 18 runs and each of the others 8; Janet had a second seed at every rate from 6e-3 up, the others only at their two best. More runs give more chances of a lucky minimum, so the comparison slightly favours Janet.
  • Selected second seeds: the second seed's rates were chosen from the first seed's results, so each two-seed mean at a best rate carries part of the first seed's selection, equally for every block.
  • Two seeds show trends, not significance. GeGLU's best two-seed mean, 2.084 at 3e-3, could move by about ±0.02; its single run at 4e-3 scored 2.078.
  • The classic blocks were not pushed to their breaking point; above 8e-3 their behaviour is not measured.
  • Inactive units are counted on 8,192 positions with a 0.5% threshold, and only for Janet and ReLU². With four layers, "the middle layers" are two of four.
  • The training settings are the training program's defaults, set during Janet's development, and not tuned for any block.
  • The step time is the whole model's, on the project's own training program and one integrated GPU; it is not an inference measurement.

Next

Further measurements will follow, so that the comparison is more precise and fair:

  • Equal runs for every block: the same grid of rates, extended past 8e-3 for the classic blocks; the same two seeds at every rate, three or more at the best; every block taken to its breaking point.
  • Weight sizes through training, to test the third explanation.
  • One rate for the feed-forward block and another for the rest, to test the fourth.
  • A remedy against inactive units, to see whether it widens the safe region without costing loss at the optimum.
  • A larger model and a longer run.

The machine running these measurements is shared with other work, so they will come in stages.
